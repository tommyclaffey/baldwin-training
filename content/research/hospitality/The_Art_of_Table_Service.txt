THE ART OF
Table Service
SERVICE TRAINING MODULE
 
Service is what we do; hospitality is how it makes the guest feel. Drawing on the philosophies of Danny Meyer (Setting the Table) and Will Guidara (Unreasonable Hospitality), this module treats every table as a conversation, one where we are always on the guest’s side, and where small, personal gestures become the moments guests remember and retell.
1  Mise en Place
Everything in its place before the doors open. A prepared server is free to be fully present with guests, because nothing pulls attention away from them.
	•	Station: Glassware and silver polished and spotless; linens pressed; side stand stocked with napkins, crumber, wine key, pens, candles and water service pieces.
	•	Knowledge: Taste the specials; know ingredients, allergens, the 86 list, and the wine, cocktail and water offerings well enough to describe them in a sentence.
	•	Pre-shift: Review reservations and guest notes: regulars, celebrations, dietary needs and VIPs, so we can plan a gesture before the guest arrives.
	•	Self: Uniform pressed, grooming immaculate, phone away. Reset your mind and your station between every table.

2  Open Hand Service
Gesture and present with an open, relaxed palm, never a pointed finger: toward the menu, a seat, the restroom, a dish. Keep hands visible and out of pockets. An open hand says “welcome” before a word is spoken.

3  Posture
Stand tall with shoulders relaxed and weight balanced. Never lean on chairs, side stands or tables. At the table, stay calm and still, hands relaxed, bending slightly to speak with seated guests without ever hovering over them.
4  Smiling
A real smile reaches the eyes. Smile as you approach, before you speak, and when you say goodbye. Between tables, shake off any stress; each guest deserves a fresh, unhurried version of you.

5  Vocal Tone and Pace
Speak warmly, a little lower and a touch slower than ordinary conversation, and match the table’s energy: quiet for an intimate dinner, brighter for a celebration. Prefer “Absolutely,” “Certainly” and “My pleasure” over “No problem” or “you guys.”
6  Sincerity
Guests sense performance instantly. Be genuinely curious, listen to the answers, and use what you learn. If you love a dish, say why. “I’m not sure, let me find out” always beats a guess. Noticing is where every great gesture begins.

7  Moving with Grace
Walk with purpose, never rush, never run. Carry plates with two hands and a steady core, serve from the guest’s side without reaching across them, and yield the aisle. Scan every table on every pass; never travel empty-handed.

10  Suggestive Selling
A great suggestion is simply help: guiding the guest toward the best possible meal. Revenue follows naturally from trust.
	•	Ask first, then suggest: Learn what they lean toward (light or rich, adventurous or classic) before you recommend.
	•	Offer two, not ten: Share a personal favorite and describe it in sensory words; a short, confident answer beats a long list.
	•	Think in pairings: Connect cocktails, wine, courses and dessert so each suggestion completes the one before it.
	•	Read the table: Celebrations welcome a toast; a quiet dinner may want a lighter touch. Never pressure, and honor every “no” graciously.
Say it: “The crudo is what I’d begin with tonight, bright and delicate. It sets up the halibut beautifully, and a glass of the Chablis is lovely alongside both.”

11  Coursing
Coursing is the rhythm of the meal. Guests should never feel rushed or forgotten.
	•	Order and communicate: Enter every item by course and tell the kitchen about any special pacing or timing request.
	•	Ask about pace: “Would you like us to linger, or do you have a curtain to catch?” Adjust the whole meal to the answer.
	•	Serve together: Present each course to the whole table at the same moment, describing every dish. Clear only when every guest has finished.
	•	Check back early: Return within the first couple of bites, then give the table space to enjoy it.
12  Manicuring the Table
The table is our canvas, and it is never allowed to look used. Tend it quietly, between and during courses.
	•	Clear and reset: Remove used items silently and align the next course’s cutlery, glassware and linens perfectly straight.
	•	Crumb before dessert: Using a crumber or folded napkin and a small plate held just below the table edge, sweep lightly toward the plate.
	•	Little details: Straighten candles and décor, refold napkins when a guest steps away, wipe spills discreetly, and replace soiled linen.
13  Closing the Check & Heartfelt Farewell
The final moments shape what guests carry home. Make them as intentional as the first.
	•	Never rush: Present the check when requested or when the table signals, with “No rush at all, whenever you’re ready.” Process payment promptly.
	•	Make it personal: Thank them by name, and refer to something you learned, such as their occasion or a favorite dish.
	•	Walk them out: Whenever possible, escort guests to the door and invite them back warmly; add notes to their profile for next time.
Say it: “It was a true pleasure having you tonight. Happy anniversary, and we can’t wait to welcome you back.”
The measure of our service: did every guest feel that we were on their side?
